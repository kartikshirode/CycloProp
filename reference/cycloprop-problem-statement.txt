India is entering a new era of autonomous aerial systems, intelligent mobility,
and drone-enabled digital infrastructure. The PUSHPAK Grand Challenge 2026 is
a national platform created to identify and accelerate high-impact engineering
solutions that can address real-world problems through drones, AI, robotics,
sensing, communication systems, and autonomous technologies.
This is not a conventional hackathon or idea competition. The Grand Challenge is
designed to take promising technical concepts through evaluation, mentoring,
prototyping, testing, and deployment-oriented development. Selected teams will
work with researchers, domain experts, and industry mentors to transform
innovative ideas into functional, field-ready prototypes.

Objective:

The objective of the Grand Challenge is to support the detailed, CAE-supported
design of an indigenous cycloidal rotor module for drone applications.

The challenge will focus on developing technically sound cyclorotor designs
through kinematic, aerodynamic, structural, and integration-level analysis. The
end goal of the Grand Challenge is not immediate prototype fabrication, but the
identification of strong, build-ready cyclorotor designs.

Challenge Question:

Cycloidal rotors offer a promising alternative to conventional drone propulsion
systems by enabling rapid thrust vectoring, improved maneuverability, and
enhanced control authority. Unlike conventional propellers, cyclorotors can
vary the direction of generated thrust through cyclic blade-pitch variation. This
capability is useful for drones operating in confined spaces, near structures, in
gusty environments, and in missions requiring precise attitude and orientation
control.

However, cyclorotor-based drone technology is not widely available in India.
Indigenous development of this technology requires focused work on blade-pitch
kinematics, rotor geometry, aerodynamic performance, structural design,
lightweight mechanisms, power requirements, and manufacturability.

The problem is to develop a detailed engineering design of a cycloidal rotor
module suitable for drone applications. The design should target at least 10 N
thrust and a thrust-to-weight ratio greater than 2.5. Teams must support their
design through CAD models, kinematic analysis, aerodynamic calculations or
simulations, structural assessment, mass estimation, material selection,
actuation strategy, and an implementation plan.

The expected submission is a detailed design package that demonstrates
technical feasibility and readiness for prototype development. The final selected
designs will be eligible for a subsequent build phase with support from IIT
Bombay.

Format of Competition:
Stage 1: Problem Statement Announcement and Preliminary
Design

The Grand Challenge will begin with the announcement of the problem
statement. Teams will be given one month to prepare and submit a preliminary
design.

The preliminary design submission should include:

    1. Cyclorotor concept and configuration.
    2. Preliminary rotor sizing.
    3. Blade arrangement and pitch-control concept.
    4. Estimated thrust and power requirement.
    5. Estimated module weight and thrust-to-weight ratio.
    6. Initial material and manufacturing approach.
    7. Team capability and execution plan.

No financial support will be provided at this stage. The objective of this stage is
to identify technically promising teams for detailed design support.

The organizing team will evaluate preliminary designs and shortlist up to 15
teams. These teams will receive Rs. 1 lakh each to develop a complete,
CAE-supported detailed design.

Stage 2: Detailed Design Development

During The detailed design phase period, shortlisted teams will prepare a full
design package including:

    1. CAD model of the complete cyclorotor module.
    2. Kinematic model of the blade-pitch mechanism.
    3. Aerodynamic analysis or simulation for thrust prediction.
    4. Structural analysis of blades, supports, frame, shaft, and critical linkages.
    5. Motor, actuator, bearing, and controller selection.
    6. Material selection and mass estimate.
    7. Thrust-to-weight ratio estimate.
    8. Manufacturability and assembly plan.
    9. Bill of materials and cost estimate.
    10.Risk assessment and mitigation plan.
    11.Build and test plan for the next phase.
After submission of detailed design reports, the organizing team will evaluate the
submissions. Up to 10 teams will be shortlisted for final presentation during
IIT Bombay Techfest in December 2026.

Stage 3: Detailed Design Evaluation

During this stage, the evaluation committee may provide brief feedback to the
shortlisted teams. Teams will be encouraged to improve their designs,
simulations, documentation, and presentation before the final round. Travel or
participation support may be provided to the shortlisted finalist teams, subject to
the approved budget norms.

Final Presentation: Techfest Evaluation

The final round will be conducted during IIT Bombay Techfest in December 2026.
Finalist teams will present their detailed design before the evaluation committee
and respond to technical questions.

The final evaluation will be based on:

    1. Detailed design report.
    2. CAD and CAE-supported technical evidence.
    3. Final presentation.
    4. Viva and technical defence.
    5. Feasibility of implementation in the next phase.

Up to 5 winning teams will be selected. These winners will receive awards or
recognition grants and will be considered for further financial support, technical
mentoring, and access to resources for prototype development and testing with
IIT Bombay support.

Team Specification and Eligibility:

Expected Target Audience (Upto 5 members per team)
The Grand Challenge is intended for teams capable of developing a technically
rigorous cyclorotor design supported by CAD, simulation, and engineering
analysis.

The expected target audience includes:

    1. Academic institutions working in UAVs, rotorcraft, aerodynamics, robotics,
         controls, or mechanical design.

    2. Faculty-led student teams with strong design, simulation, and prototyping
         capability.

    3. Startups developing drone platforms, propulsion systems, robotics
         hardware, or UAV subsystems.

    4. MSMEs with capability in precision fabrication, lightweight structures,
         electric propulsion, embedded systems, or drone components.

    5. Academia­startup­industry consortia interested in developing advanced
         indigenous UAV technologies.

Preference may be given to teams with demonstrated capability in one or more
of the following areas:

    1. Rotor design and unsteady aerodynamics.
    2. CAD modelling and mechanical design.
    3. Kinematic analysis of mechanisms.
    4. CFD, FEA, multibody dynamics, or other CAE tools.
    5. Lightweight structures and material selection.
    6. Motor, actuator, and control-system selection.
    7. UAV subsystem integration and testing.
